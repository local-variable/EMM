## Resolution

The graph is **a view over the pricing engine, not a chart of prices**: the line on it is the same estimate the pricer quotes, evaluated day by day, and every honesty rule the estimator carries — sample gating, provisional grading, Freshness, Sample — is drawn rather than footnoted. "Confidence bars" resolves to **two bands that cannot be confused, and a third that must not exist.**

Worked as a visual prototype against real market data: fifteen Wares from the cached Cactuar corpus spanning a liquid materia, a consumable that trades in both Qualities, crafted gear with HQ dominant, furnishings across a regime change, a multi-million-gil minion, an Item with Listings but no Sales and a stale upload, and one with a single Sale. Every layer toggles, every regime was rendered and checked. The prototype and its design notes live at `.agent/prototypes/graph/` — local and deliberately unpublished, referenced from the continuity file so a later session can find it.

### The ticket's questions, answered

**The series — Sales, Listings, or both?** Both, but never as the same kind of mark. **Sales are dots**, one per Sale, sized by Stack, in the Quality's colour — what buyers paid. **Listings are lines**: each Listing still on the board is drawn from the moment it was put up (`lastReviewTime`) to now, plus a ladder in the right margin showing the whole board at this instant — what sellers hope for. A Listing is never a dot and never enters a band or the estimate. The trend line is neither: it is the **estimate**, the pricing engine's number evaluated once per UTC day on the Sales strictly before that day, drawn at full weight in the Quality's colour so it reads as "same Ware, but computed". Because it comes from the same code the pricer uses, the chart cannot disagree with the price.

**The band's meaning.** Two ship, drawn so they cannot be mistaken for each other:

- **Where Sales landed** — the middle 50% and middle 80% of Sales in a window around that moment. A description of dispersion. Soft nested fill; opacity rises with effective sample size.
- **Precision of the estimate** — an 80% moving-block bootstrap interval on the median: how well the number is pinned down by the Sales EMM has. Two dotted edges either side of the line — an interval, not a cloud.

A **predicted range is not drawn, and the legend says so in words**. This follows directly from the undercut-formula finding that going from under 5 to over 25 effective observations moved forward error only from 33% to 27%: an interval measures estimation error and says almost nothing about the next Sale, so a "prediction band" would be a claim the chart cannot back. Both bands answer real seller questions — "how spread out are prices right now?" and "how sure is EMM of its number?" — and on a regime change they diverge visibly: the precision interval balloons even with many Sales in the window, which is exactly the moment the reader should not trust the point.

**Sparse-data honesty.** Six mechanisms, all rendered: dots are always drawn, so three Sales look like three dots and three hundred look like a cloud; the dispersion band is **absent below 5 effective observations** (its window widens automatically toward the ware type's estimation window first, and fades as evidence thins); the estimate line **breaks where no Sales rung applies and is dashed where provisional**; candles, offered as an optional overlay for the stock-chart-literate, draw a bucket with fewer than three Sales as a tick and never a body; **Freshness is on the time axis** — the span since the last upload is hatched and labelled "no upload for …"; and the start of held History is marked ("History begins here"). The stats card carries the severity ladder — `ok` / `caution` / `warning` / `severe` — as a chip and a warning box, and the "not a forecast" sentence sits beside the interval.

**HQ vs NQ.** An **overlay, chosen by a three-way NQ · HQ · Both control — never a facet**. They are two Wares with two estimates and two bands, but the question a seller asks is "what is the HQ premium and is it moving?", which needs one axis. Colours are fixed to the entity (NQ blue, HQ orange, both themes) whatever is toggled; the control is disabled for Items that cannot be HQ.

**Time axis and range.** Presets 7d · 14d · 30d · 60d · 90d · 180d · All, bucket Auto or forced (6h · 1d · 3d · 1w). **The default opens on the window the estimate was priced from** — 30 days for most types, 60 for gear, rounded up to a preset — so the picture and the number agree, and the estimation window is shaded on longer views. "All" is bounded only by what EMM has stored; the aggregator holds multi-year, second-resolution history.

**Rendering.** **ImPlot, through the binding Dalamud already ships — no new dependency.** Verified on the development machine: `Dalamud.Bindings.ImPlot.dll` and `cimplot.dll` sit beside `Dalamud.dll` at the same commit as the running API-15 build, and the native library reports **ImPlot 0.14**. Every primitive the design needs is present — subplots with linked axes, scatter, shaded, line, stairs, bars, error bars, annotations, axis tags, drag lines, log and time scales, and the plot draw list for what ImPlot has no built-in for (per-point radius, hollow/filled candles, the act glyphs, the hatch, the margin ladder). Licences read from the primary sources: **ImPlot MIT, cimplot MIT** — both compatible with the repository's GPL-3.0. Fully hand-drawn rendering was considered and rejected: axes, zoom, ticks, legends and hover are what ImPlot does well, and the custom marks compose on its draw list. **New requirement recorded on approval: the graph must be able to pull up in an item overlay when called upon** — invocable per Ware from wherever the Player is looking, not only from a fixed dashboard pane. That is information architecture and is handed to the UI ticket.

### What the prototype adds beyond the six questions

- **The Player's acts on the same axis** — ▲ List, ▼ Reprice, ★ Sold, ‖ Hold, with runs of Holds collapsed into one labelled span in an acts lane, and a Listing drawn as a shelf from List to Sold. In the product these come from the Proposal Ledger, per the core-feature-set decision. The prototype had no Ledger to draw from and stood in a **Strategy replayed over real History**; on review that replay was **dropped as a feature — not useful to the end user** — so what ships is the Player's own recorded acts, not a hypothetical.
- **Volume** (units sold per bucket, per Quality) and **Spread** (the middle 50% of Sales as a share of the median — a percentile analogue of a volatility band, because these distributions are heavy-tailed) as sub-panes on the shared axis. Volume, volatility, Sales and trend are on screen at once, which was the brief.
- **Days of Supply** as the headline number in the stats card and, from the ladder, "N units below the estimate ≈ D days to clear" — time-to-clear where the reader is already looking. Its *history* pane is off by default: see below.
- A stats card in the lineage of the item-page references — estimate as hero, Sample, Freshness, medians at three windows, sold per day, the board, Min Listing, listed below the estimate, the Data Centre average labelled as a Scope you would travel to, vendor prices, Stack, ware type with its window and horizon.

### What was learned, for review

- **The estimator is a trailing statistic and the graph shows its lag.** On one furnishing the market moved 80k → 15k → 70k → 40k across ninety days; the 30-day trailing median crosses each regime change roughly half a window late, and its precision interval balloons during the transition even with 27 Sales in the window. That is the estimator behaving correctly, and it gives the safety-posture ticket's "AR/MA over History" clause a concrete target — a level-shift-aware component, or a window the daily refit may shorten. Flagged, not decided.
- **`lastReviewTime` back-projection is a lower bound, not a series.** Only Listings still on the board can be traced back; those that sold since are invisible, so reconstructed depth is understated and rises spuriously toward the present. The Listing shelf lines survive as a layer regardless — a 2M Listing sitting forty days above the Sale cloud reads instantly — but **Days of Supply history needs EMM's own stored Snapshots**, which accrue from install day. Handed to the ingestion and caching ticket: Snapshot history is a first-class store, not a cache.
- **A palette that looked fine failed the colour-vision check.** The first choice for the Player's acts (violet) was indistinguishable from the NQ blue for protan vision on the dark surface (ΔE 1.9); aqua passes all-pairs in both modes. Acts also carry a shape and a label, never colour alone.

### Ruled on review

- **Candles stay as an optional overlay**, never the primary mark, with the tick-only rule under three Sales.
- **The dispersion band stays centred** (it describes) while the estimate trails (it prices); the legend labels each.
- **"Estimate" enters the glossary** — the pricing engine's fitted Unit Price for a Ware, computed rather than observed, which is what separates it from a Reference Price; always shown with its Sample, its precision interval and a severity grade, provisional and graded rather than withheld, and never a forecast.
- **The Days of Supply history pane ships off by default** until EMM's own Snapshots make its history real.
- **The Strategy replay is dropped** as above.
- **The layer controls are to be improved beyond the prototype for better understanding** — clearer naming, grouping and explanation of what each layer is — a direction for the build rather than a fixed default set.

Still open: the warning strings shown in the severity box are the undercut-formula ticket's unapproved copy.
